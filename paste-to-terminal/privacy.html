Paste to Terminal – for iTerm2 · Last updated 2026-10-12

Paste to Terminal is a Safari extension that sends text you select on a web page into an iTerm2 tab on your Mac.

What Paste to Terminal collects

Nothing. It does not collect, store on any server, sell or share any personal data. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, and it makes no network requests.

What it handles on your Mac

  • The text you choose to send. When you pick a tab from the menu, the selected text goes from Safari to iTerm2 on your Mac. It isn't saved anywhere and isn't sent anywhere else.
  • Your iTerm2 window and tab names. Read to build the menu, and kept only in memory while Safari is open.

Paste to Terminal does not use Safari's extension storage and keeps no history.

Website access

Safari asks you to allow Paste to Terminal on websites. It needs this to read the text you selected and to check whether part of it is hidden on the page. It only looks at your selection: while you select, it notes whether anything is selected (to keep the menu up to date), and when you right-click it checks the selected text. It does not read or send the rest of the page.

Control of iTerm2

macOS asks you to let Paste to Terminal control iTerm2. It uses this only to list your windows and tabs and to paste the text you chose. It never runs commands. You can turn this off in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Automation.
